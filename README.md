## Three kinds of "promo code," and only one survives renewal

This is where most price comparisons go wrong. A 40% recurring code, a 3-month intro rate, and a $200 credit look similar in a headline and behave nothing alike.

| Mechanism | What you pay in year 1 | What you pay in year 2 | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Recurring discount | Reduced rate every cycle | Same reduced rate | Plan eligibility, one code per order |
| Intro pricing | Low for the first months, then list price | Full list price | Bill jumps mid-term |
| New-user credit | Credit offsets early usage | Nothing left | Credit expires, often in 60 days |

Concrete example of intro pricing, from a provider currently advertising it: US plans at $2/$5/$8/$14/$20 for the first three months of a 1-year term, then $6/$14/$25/$47/$68. On the $2 tier that's 3 × $2 + 9 × $6 = **$60** for the year, against $72 at the flat $6 rate, a saving of $12. On the $20 tier it's 3 × $20 + 9 × $68 = **$672** against $816. The headline reads "from $2," the actual first-year outlay is $60, and year two is $72. No code is involved in that offer at all.

Credits behave differently again. A $200 or $300 new-user credit is typically issued automatically through a signup link, requires no code, and expires 60 days after it's claimed. It reduces what you pay in the first two months. It does not change the list price, and it does nothing for renewal.

A recurring percentage code is the only mechanism that changes the number on every invoice. That's the category Evoxt's circulating codes belong to.

## Five checks before you pay

**1. One code per order.** Stacking two percentage codes on a single invoice isn't supported by mainstream VPS billing systems, and Evoxt's checkout has one Promotional Code field. Pick the larger discount and move on.

**2. Read the renewal line, not the headline.** On a recurring code the renewal equals the first invoice, which is the whole point. On intro pricing, calculate the post-intro bill yourself: multiply the intro rate by the intro months, the standard rate by the remaining months, and add. If the annual total isn't meaningfully below the flat rate, the "deal" is a cash-flow trick.

**3. Confirm your plan is in scope.** Discounts are usually tied to a product group or a minimum tier. VM-0.5 sitting outside the percentage discount is a normal arrangement, not a bait-and-switch.

**4. Check whether the benefit is a discount or a credit.** Credits commonly expire 60 days after being claimed. A $300 credit on a $6/month server is five months of free service if you use it inside the window, and worth nothing if you sign up in November planning to deploy in February.

**5. Work the failure list in order.** Typo in the code, plan not eligible, code expired, stale browser session, then support. Clearing cache or switching browsers fixes a surprising share of "the code doesn't work" cases, and it costs 30 seconds. If a code from a coupon page fails all five, it's probably retired; coupon aggregators keep dead entries listed for years, complete with counters.

Some providers advertise all ports open and rDNS/PTR control as a feature; that's the thing to look for if this is your use case.

## FAQ

**Can I stack two Evoxt promo codes?**
No. One Promotional Code field, one code per order. Choose the one with the larger effect on your billing cycle.

**Do the codes work for existing customers?**
Recurring codes are generally aimed at new signups, and the reported Evoxt codes are described in that context. Existing customers occasionally find upgrade or renewal promotions inside the billing panel, especially around Black Friday and New Year. Ask support directly if you're mid-term and want to apply a current code.

**Should I take the discount or a new-user credit if both are offered?**
The discount, if it's genuinely recurring and you plan to stay more than two months. A 60-day credit is worth more in month one and nothing afterward.

**Monthly or annual billing?**
With a recurring code, monthly costs the same per month as annual and keeps your exit open, at the price of paying twelve times instead of once. Annual makes sense when the provider offers an additional prepay discount or when you've already run the workload for a month and know it fits.

**What's included that usually costs extra?**
Weekly offsite backup on every plan, IPv4 and IPv6, and firewall rules configurable from the control panel without connecting to the VM. Backups are the line item most budget hosts upsell, so it's worth checking on any provider you compare against.
